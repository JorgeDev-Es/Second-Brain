# 'ei'
In German, the spelling "ei" consistently represents a **diphthong**. A diphthong is a vowel sound that involves a glide from one vowel quality to another within the same syllable.

- **IPA Symbol:** The sound is typically transcribed as the **open-mid front unrounded vowel** followed by a **close front unrounded vowel** **/aɪ/**. This means it starts with a sound similar to the "a" in "cat" or "father" and glides towards the "i" in "sit" or "see."
    
- **How it's produced:**
    
    1. **Start:** Your tongue is relatively low and forward in your mouth.
    2. **Glide:** Your tongue then moves upwards and forwards, almost to the position for an "ee" sound.
    3. **Unrounded:** Your lips remain unrounded throughout the entire glide.
- **Comparison to English:**
    
    - This German "ei" sound is very similar to the diphthong found in English words like **"my," "buy," "light,"** or the "i" in **"ice."** If you can pronounce the English "long I" sound, you're very close to the German "ei."
- **Examples:**
    
    - **ein** (a/an, one) → /aɪn/
    - **heiß** (hot) → /haɪs/
    - **klein** (small) → /klaɪn/
    - **mein** (my) → /maɪn/
    - **Zeit** (time) → /tsaɪt/
- **Contrast with "ie" and "ei" confusion:** It's crucial not to confuse "ei" (/aɪ/) with "ie" (/iː/). This is a common source of error for learners, as the two spellings look similar but produce completely different vowel sounds.
    
    - "ie" = **long "e" sound** (like English "see")
    - "ei" = **long "i" sound** (like English "my")
    
    Thinking of them in terms of their English equivalents can be helpful:
    
    - "ie" (German) ≈ "ee" (English)
    - "ei" (German) ≈ "eye" (English)
