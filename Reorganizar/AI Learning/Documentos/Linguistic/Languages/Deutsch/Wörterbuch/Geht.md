# Geht

The word **Geht** is the third-person singular present indicative form of the German verb "gehen," meaning "to go" or "to walk," and is also widely used in idiomatic expressions.

## Meaning

### To go or to walk

This is the literal meaning of **geht**, referring to the act of moving from one place to another on foot.

#### Examples

> "Er geht zur Arbeit." This means "He walks to work." "Das Kind geht schon." This means "The child is already walking."

### To be doing (as in "How are you doing?")

When used in phrases like "Wie geht's?" (short for "Wie geht es?"), **geht** functions impersonally to inquire about someone's well-being or how things are progressing.

#### Examples

> "Wie geht's dir?" This means "How are you doing?" or "How's it going for you?" "Es geht mir gut." This means "I am doing well." (Literally, "It goes well for me.")

### To work or to function

**Geht** can also mean that something is working, functioning, or possible.

#### Examples

> "Das Telefon geht nicht." This means "The phone isn't working." "Das geht in Ordnung." This means "That's fine" or "That's acceptable."

### To be possible

Used impersonally, **geht** can indicate feasibility or possibility.

#### Examples

> "Das geht leider nicht." This means "Unfortunately, that's not possible." "Ich zeige dir, wie es geht." This means "I'll show you how it's done/possible."

## Phonetic and pronunciation

The pronunciation of **geht** is fairly consistent.

- **Pronunciation:** /ɡeːt/
- **Sounds:**
    - The "g" is a hard "g" sound, like in "go."
    - The "e" is a long "e" sound, similar to the "ay" in "say" but slightly longer, or the "e" in "gehen."
    - The "t" is a voiceless alveolar stop, like in "take."

## Synonyms

The synonyms for "geht" depend heavily on the specific meaning being conveyed.

### Synonyms for "to go/walk"

- **Laufen:** (to run, but also colloquially to walk, especially if moving fast)
    - _Example:_ Er **läuft** zum Bus. (He runs/walks quickly to the bus.)
- **Marschieren:** (to march)
    - _Example:_ Die Soldaten **marschieren**. (The soldiers are marching.)
- **Schreiten:** (to stride, to step, often with a more formal or deliberate connotation)
    - _Example:_ Sie **schreitet** langsam durch den Saal. (She walks slowly through the hall.)

### Synonyms for "to function/work"

- **Funktionieren:** (to function, to work - typically for machines or systems)
    - _Example:_ Der Motor **funktioniert** nicht. (The engine isn't working.)
- **Laufen:** (to run, to operate - also for machines or processes)
    - _Example:_ Der Computer **läuft** gut. (The computer is running well.)
- **Klar gehen:** (to be okay, to be fine, informal)
    - _Example:_ Das **geht klar**. (That's okay/fine.)

### Synonyms for "to be possible"

- **Möglich sein:** (to be possible)
    - _Example:_ Das ist **möglich**. (That is possible.)
- **Machbar sein:** (to be feasible, doable)
    - _Example:_ Das ist **machbar**. (That is feasible.)

## Morphological features

**Geht** is an inflected form of the strong (irregular) verb **gehen**.

- **Base form (infinitive):** gehen
- **Verb class:** Strong verb
- **Person:** Third person
- **Number:** Singular
- **Tense:** Present
- **Mood:** Indicative
- **Conjugation pattern:**
    - ich gehe
    - du gehst
    - er/sie/es **geht**
    - wir gehen
    - ihr geht
    - sie/Sie gehen

## Lexical equivalents

The word **geht** has lexical equivalents in many languages, depending on the context.

### English

The word **geht** has lexical equivalents like "goes," "walks," "is doing" (in "how's it going?"), "works," or "is possible."

### Spanish

The word **geht** has lexical equivalents like "va" (from "ir" - to go), "camina" (from "caminar" - to walk), "está" (in "cómo está?" - how is he/she/it?), or "funciona" (from "funcionar" - to function).

### French

The word **geht** has lexical equivalents like "va" (from "aller" - to go), "marche" (from "marcher" - to walk, or "to work/function"), or "est possible" (is possible).

### Italian

The word **geht** has lexical equivalents like "va" (from "andare" - to go), "cammina" (from "camminare" - to walk), "funziona" (from "funzionare" - to function), or "è possibile" (is possible).
