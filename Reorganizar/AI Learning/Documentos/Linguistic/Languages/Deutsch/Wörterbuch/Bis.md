# Bis

The German word **bis** is a versatile preposition and conjunction that primarily means "until," "by," "up to," or "to," indicating a limit in time, place, or quantity.

## Meaning

### Until or by (time)

This is one of the most common uses, indicating a point in time up to which something occurs or is due.

#### Examples

> "Ich warte **bis** morgen." This means "I'll wait until tomorrow." "Die Frist ist **bis** Freitag." This means "The deadline is by Friday."

### Up to or as far as (place/extent)

**Bis** can also indicate a spatial limit or extent, meaning "up to" or "as far as."

#### Examples

> "Der Bus fährt **bis** zum Bahnhof." This means "The bus goes up to/as far as the train station." "Das Wasser reicht mir **bis** zu den Knien." This means "The water reaches me up to my knees."

### To or for (purpose/destination, often with "zu" or "nach")

When indicating a destination or purpose, **bis** is often combined with other prepositions like "zu" or "nach."

#### Examples

> "Ich fahre **bis** nach Berlin." This means "I'm driving to Berlin." (Emphasizes the completion of the journey to Berlin) "**Bis** auf Weiteres." This means "Until further notice."

### Expressing a range or limit

**Bis** can be used to set a range or limit for numbers or quantities.

#### Examples

> "Zähle von eins **bis** zehn." This means "Count from one to ten." "Das Buch kostet 10 **bis** 15 Euro." This means "The book costs 10 to 15 Euros."

### Short for "Auf Wiedersehen" (in farewells)

In informal farewells, **bis** is often used in combination with adverbs of time to mean "until [time/event]," such as in "Bis bald" (See you soon) or "Bis später" (See you later).

#### Examples

> "**Bis** bald!" This means "See you soon!" "**Bis** morgen!" This means "See you tomorrow!"

## Phonetic and pronunciation

The pronunciation of **bis** is straightforward.

- **Pronunciation:** /bɪs/
- **Sounds:**
    - The "b" sound is a voiced bilabial stop, like in "bed."
    - The "i" sound is a short, lax "i," similar to the "i" in "sit" or "Himmel."
    - The "s" sound is a voiceless alveolar fricative, like in "snake."

## Synonyms

The synonyms for "bis" depend heavily on the specific context and nuance.

### Synonyms for "until/by (time)"

- **Bis zu:** (up to, until - often used with an article)
    - _Example:_ Wir haben Zeit **bis zum** Abend. (We have time until the evening.)
- **Vor:** (before - can imply a deadline similar to "by")
    - _Example:_ Bitte erledigen Sie das **vor** Freitag. (Please complete that by Friday.)

### Synonyms for "up to/as far as (place/extent)"

- **Bis zu:** (up to, as far as - usually with an article)
    - _Example:_ Ich gehe **bis zur** Kirche. (I'm going up to/as far as the church.)
- **Nach:** (to, towards - for towns, countries, points of the compass, or home)
    - _Example:_ Ich fahre **nach** Berlin. (I'm driving to Berlin.)
- **Zu:** (to, towards - for people, institutions, or specific places)
    - _Example:_ Ich gehe **zum** Arzt. (I'm going to the doctor.)

## Morphological features

**Bis** is classified as a **preposition** and sometimes as a **conjunction** when it introduces a subordinate clause. It generally takes the **accusative case** when indicating a limit in time or space, but it also combines frequently with other prepositions (like _zu_ or _nach_), which then determine the case (often dative).

## Lexical equivalents

The word **bis** has lexical equivalents in many languages, primarily translating to "until," "by," or "up to."

### English

The word **bis** has lexical equivalents like "until," "till," "by," "up to," "as far as," or "to."

### Spanish

The word **bis** has lexical equivalents like "hasta," "a" (in phrases like "de uno a diez").

### French

The word **bis** has lexical equivalents like "jusqu'à," "à" (in some ranges).

### Italian

The word **bis** has lexical equivalents like "fino a," "entro" (for deadlines), "a" (in some ranges).
