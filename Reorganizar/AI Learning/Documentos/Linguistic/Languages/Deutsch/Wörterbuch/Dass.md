# Dass

The German word **dass** is a subordinating conjunction that primarily means "that," introducing a subordinate clause. It functions similarly to "that" in English when it introduces a noun clause or a clause of purpose/result.

## Meaning

### That (introducing a subordinate clause)

This is the most common use of **dass**, where it introduces a dependent clause that provides more information about the main clause. The verb in the subordinate clause typically moves to the end.

#### Examples

> "Ich weiß, **dass** du Recht hast." This means "I know that you are right." "Es ist wichtig, **dass** du kommst." This means "It is important that you come."

### So that/in order that (indicating purpose or result, often with "so")

While "damit" is more common for explicit purpose, **dass** can also indicate a result or purpose, especially when used in conjunction with "so" (so...that).

#### Examples

> "Er hat so viel gegessen, **dass** ihm schlecht wurde." This means "He ate so much that he felt sick." "Sei vorsichtig, **dass** du nicht fällst." This means "Be careful so that you don't fall." (Though "damit" would also fit here, "dass" is possible.)

## Phonetic and pronunciation

The pronunciation of **dass** is straightforward.

- **Pronunciation:** /das/
- **Sounds:**
    - The "d" sound is a voiced alveolar stop, like in "dog."
    - The "a" sound is a short, open "a," similar to the "a" in "cat" or "Mann."
    - The "ss" indicates a voiceless alveolar fricative, like the "s" in "kiss."

## Synonyms

While **dass** is quite specific in its function as a subordinating conjunction, other conjunctions can sometimes be used to introduce clauses with similar functions, though often with slightly different nuances.

### Conjunctions for introducing clauses

- **Ob:** (whether, if - introduces indirect questions)
    - _Example:_ Ich frage mich, **ob** er kommt. (I wonder whether he's coming.) - _Not a direct synonym, but also introduces a dependent clause._
- **Damit:** (so that, in order that - specifically for purpose)
    - _Example:_ Ich lerne, **damit** ich die Prüfung bestehe. (I'm studying so that I pass the exam.) - _Used for purpose, where "dass" might sometimes be used for result._
- **Als:** (when, as - for past single events)
    - _Example:_ **Als** ich jung war, ... (When I was young, ...) - _Not a synonym, but another common subordinating conjunction._
- **Wenn:** (when, if - for recurring events or conditions)
    - _Example:_ **Wenn** es regnet, bleibe ich zu Hause. (If it rains, I stay at home.) - _Also a common subordinating conjunction._

## Morphological features

**Dass** is a **subordinating conjunction**. It is indeclinable and invariable, meaning its form does not change. Its primary grammatical function is to introduce a dependent clause.

## Lexical equivalents

The word **dass** has direct lexical equivalents in many languages, primarily meaning "that."

### English

The word **dass** has the lexical equivalent "that" (as a conjunction).

### Spanish

The word **dass** has the lexical equivalent "que."

### French

The word **dass** has the lexical equivalent "que."

### Italian

The word **dass** has the lexical equivalent "che."
