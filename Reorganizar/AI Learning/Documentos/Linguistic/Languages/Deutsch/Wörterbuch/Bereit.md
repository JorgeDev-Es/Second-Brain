# Bereit

The word **Bereit** is a German adjective that generally means "ready" or "prepared," but can also convey a sense of "willingness."

## Meaning

### Ready/Prepared

This usage of **Bereit** indicates that something or someone is in a state of readiness or has completed the necessary preparations for an action, event, or situation.

#### Examples

> "Der Zug ist zur Abfahrt **bereit**." (The train is ready for departure.) "Ich bin **bereit** für die Prüfung." (I am prepared for the exam.) "Alles ist **bereit**." (Everything is ready.)

### Willing

When used in this sense, **Bereit** signifies that someone is agreeable or disposed to do something, often implying a cooperative attitude. It often appears with the preposition "zu" + infinitive.

#### Examples

> "Wir sind zu Kompromissen **bereit**." (We are willing to make compromises.) "Er ist **bereit**, uns zu helfen." (He is willing to help us.) "Ich bin **bereit**, zuzuhören." (I am willing to listen.)

## Phonetic and pronunciation

The pronunciation of **Bereit** is:

- **Pronunciation:** /bəˈraɪ̯t/
- **Sounds:**     - The "b" sounds like the "b" in "book."     - The "e" in the first syllable is a schwa sound, like the "a" in "about."     - The "rai" sounds like the "rye" in "rye bread."     - The "t" sounds like the "t" in "tea."

There is no significant weak form for **Bereit** as it is an adjective and its pronunciation remains fairly consistent.

## Synonyms

When **Bereit** means "ready" or "prepared":

- **Fertig:** This is a very common synonym for "ready" or "finished," especially for things that are completed or set.     - _Example:_ Das Essen ist **fertig**. (The food is ready/finished.)
- **Parat:** (Colloquial) Similar to "ready" or "at hand," often used for things that are immediately available.     - _Example:_ Ich habe die Unterlagen **parat**. (I have the documents ready/at hand.)
- **Einsatzbereit:** Specifically means "ready for action" or "operational."     - _Example:_ Die Truppen sind **einsatzbereit**. (The troops are ready for action.)
- **Vorbereitet:** This emphasizes the process of preparation.     - _Example:_ Ich bin gut **vorbereitet**. (I am well prepared.)

When **Bereit** means "willing":

- **Gewillt:** (More formal) Expresses a strong will or intention to do something.     - _Example:_ Er ist **gewillt**, seine Fehler zu korrigieren. (He is willing to correct his mistakes.)
- **Geneigt:** (More formal) Implies a predisposition or inclination to do something.     - _Example:_ Sie ist **geneigt**, ihm zu verzeihen. (She is inclined to forgive him.)
- **Willens:** (Less common) Expresses a strong will or determination.     - _Example:_ Ich bin **willens**, alles zu tun. (I am willing to do anything.)

## Lexical equivalent

The word **Bereit** has lexical equivalents in many languages.

### Spanish

The word **Bereit** has lexical equivalents like "listo" (ready), "preparado" (prepared), and "dispuesto" (willing).

### French

The word **Bereit** has lexical equivalents like "prêt" (ready/prepared) and "disposé" (willing).

### Italian

The word **Bereit** has lexical equivalents like "pronto" (ready/prepared) and "disposto" (willing).
