# Bist

The word **Bist** is a conjugated form of the German verb "sein" (to be), specifically the second-person singular present tense, meaning "are" when referring to "you" (singular, informal).

## Meaning

### Are (second-person singular, informal)

This usage of **Bist** refers to the informal "you are," used when speaking to a single person you know well, or who is younger than you.

#### Examples

> "**Du bist** müde." (You are tired.) "**Bist du** zu Hause?" (Are you at home?) "**Du bist** mein Freund." (You are my friend.)

## Phonetic and pronunciation

The pronunciation of **Bist** is generally consistent.

- **Pronunciation:** /bɪst/
- **Sounds:**     - The "b" sounds like the "b" in "ball."     - The "i" sounds like the "i" in "sit" or "bit."     - The "st" sounds like the "st" in "fast."

There isn't a significantly different "weak form" as the word is already quite short and typically pronounced clearly.

## Synonyms

As **Bist** is a conjugation of the verb "sein" (to be), its synonyms are related to different forms of "to be" depending on the subject. However, in the context of "du bist," meaning "you are," it doesn't have direct single-word synonyms that maintain the same grammatical person and number. Instead, the concept of "being" is conveyed through other conjugations of "sein."

- **Ich bin** (I am)
- **Er/sie/es ist** (He/she/it is)
- **Wir sind** (We are)
- **Ihr seid** (You all are/informal plural)
- **Sie sind** (They are/You are formal singular/plural)

## Lexical equivalent

The word **Bist** has lexical equivalents in many languages.

### Spanish

The word **Bist** has a lexical equivalent like "eres" (from "ser," to be, second-person singular informal).

### French

The word **Bist** has a lexical equivalent like "es" (from "être," to be, second-person singular informal).

### Italian

The word **Bist** has a lexical equivalent like "sei" (from "essere," to be, second-person singular informal).
