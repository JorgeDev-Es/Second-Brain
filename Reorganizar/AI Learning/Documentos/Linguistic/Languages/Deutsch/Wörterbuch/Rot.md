# Rot

The German word **Rot** primarily means "red." It can function as an adjective describing the color or as a noun referring to the color itself. It also carries connotations related to flushed skin, political affiliations, and even revolutionary ideas.

## Meaning

### The color red

This usage of **rot** refers to the primary color, similar to the color of blood, fire, or strawberries. It is commonly used to describe objects, signs, or lights.

#### Examples

> "Das Auto ist **rot**." This means "The car is red." "Die Ampel ist **rot**." This means "The traffic light is red." "Er hat ein **rotes** Kleid." This means "She has a red dress."

### Flushed or reddened (of body parts/face)

When used this way, **rot** describes skin or a face that has turned red, often due to embarrassment, anger, effort, or a condition.

#### Examples

> "Sie wurde ganz **rot** vor Scham." This means "She turned completely red with shame." "Nach dem Laufen war sein Gesicht ganz **rot**." This means "After running, his face was completely red/flushed."

### Communist/Political

In a political context, **rot** can refer to something associated with communism or socialist movements.

#### Examples

> "Er ist ein überzeugter **Roter**." This means "He is a dedicated communist." "Die **rote** Fahne." This means "The red flag."

### Revolutionary/Metaphorical

Figuratively, **rot** can imply a revolutionary change or a breakthrough.

#### Examples

> "Die Erfindung war eine **rote** Revolution in der Technologiebranche." This means "The invention was a revolutionary breakthrough in the technology industry."

## Phonetic and pronunciation

The pronunciation of **rot** in German is:

- **IPA:** /ʀoːt/
- **Sounds:**
    - The "R" is a guttural "R" sound, produced in the back of the throat.
    - The "o" is a long, open "o" sound, similar to the "oa" in "boat" or "o" in "go" in English, but held longer.
    - The "t" is a clear, unvoiced "t" sound.

There isn't a significant distinction between "strong" and "weak" forms for adjectives like "rot" in the same way there might be for function words in English. However, its ending will change depending on its grammatical function (declension).

## Synonyms

The core meaning of **rot** as "red" doesn't have direct synonyms in German, as it is the primary word for the color. However, depending on the nuance, other words or phrases can be used to describe shades or types of red:

- **Purpurrot:** Purple-red, crimson.
- **Dunkelrot:** Dark red.
- **Hellrot:** Light red.
- **Feuerrot:** Fire red.
- **Blutrot:** Blood red.
- **Rosarot:** Pink-red (rosy).
- **Ziegelrot:** Brick red.

When describing flushed or reddened skin:

- **Errötet:** Blushed, reddened.
- **Glühend:** Glowing, fiery (can imply red-hot).

## Morphological features of the word

**Rot** is an adjective in German. Like all German adjectives, its ending changes to agree with the noun it modifies in terms of gender, number, and case. This is called adjective declension.

Here are some examples of its declension:

- **Strong declension (no article):**
    - Masculine nominative singular: **roter** (e.g., _roter Wein_ - red wine)
    - Feminine nominative singular: **rote** (e.g., _rote Rose_ - red rose)
    - Neuter nominative singular: **rotes** (e.g., _rotes Auto_ - red car)
    - Plural nominative: **rote** (e.g., _rote Äpfel_ - red apples)
- **Weak declension (with definite article like 'der, die, das'):**
    - Masculine nominative singular: **der rote** (e.g., _der rote Wein_ - the red wine)
    - Feminine nominative singular: **die rote** (e.g., _die rote Rose_ - the red rose)
    - Neuter nominative singular: **das rote** (e.g., _das rote Auto_ - the red car)
    - Plural nominative: **die roten** (e.g., _die roten Äpfel_ - the red apples)
- **Mixed declension (with indefinite article like 'ein, eine' or possessive pronouns):**
    - Masculine nominative singular: **ein roter** (e.g., _ein roter Wein_ - a red wine)
    - Feminine nominative singular: **eine rote** (e.g., _eine rote Rose_ - a red rose)
    - Neuter nominative singular: **ein rotes** (e.g., _ein rotes Auto_ - a red car)
    - Plural nominative: **(keine) roten** (e.g., _keine roten Äpfel_ - no red apples)

The comparative form is **röter** or **roter** (redder), and the superlative is **am rötesten** or **am rotesten** (reddest).
