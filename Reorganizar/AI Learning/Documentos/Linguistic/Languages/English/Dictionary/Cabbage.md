# Cabbage
The word **cabbage** primarily refers to a common round vegetable with edible leaves, but it also has several informal and sometimes offensive meanings, including slang for money, scraps of cloth kept by a tailor, or an offensive term for a severely brain-damaged person. As a verb, it can mean to steal or to form a head like a cabbage.
## Meaning
### A type of vegetable
This is the most common meaning of **cabbage**: a cultivated plant (Brassica oleracea var. capitata) with a short stem and a dense, globular head of edible leaves, typically green, white, or purple. It is eaten raw or cooked.
#### Examples
> "I made coleslaw with fresh **cabbage**." This means coleslaw made with the vegetable. "The garden is full of thriving **cabbage** plants." This refers to the plants growing in the garden.
### Offensive term for a severely brain-damaged person
Used informally and offensively, **cabbage** can refer to a person who is severely impaired mentally and physically, often due to brain damage, and is dependent on others for subsistence. This usage implies a state of being like a "vegetable."
#### Examples
> "After the accident, he was unfortunately described as a **cabbage**." This is an offensive way to describe someone with severe mental and physical impairment.
### Slang for money
In some informal contexts, particularly in British English, **cabbage** can be slang for paper money or cash.
#### Examples
> "He's trying to earn some extra **cabbage** this weekend." This means he's trying to earn extra money.
### (British, obsolete) Scraps of cloth
Historically, and now largely obsolete in British English, **cabbage** referred to the pieces of cloth left over after a tailor had cut out a garment, which they traditionally kept as a perquisite.
#### Examples
> "The tailor claimed the **cabbage** from the customer's material." This refers to the tailor taking the leftover fabric scraps.
### To steal or pilfer (verb)
As a verb, **cabbage** means to steal or pilfer, often referring to taking small items or scraps. This meaning is also largely British and sometimes obsolete.
#### Examples
> "He was caught trying to **cabbage** some supplies from the storeroom." This means he was caught trying to steal some supplies.
### To form a head (verb)
(Of plants) to form a head like that of a cabbage.
#### Examples
> "The lettuce will **cabbage** in a few weeks." This means the lettuce will develop a compact head.
## Phonetic and pronunciation
The pronunciation of **cabbage** is /ˈkæbɪdʒ/ (American English) or /ˈkæbɪdʒ/ (British English).
- **Sounds:**
    - The "ca" sounds like the "cab" in "cab."
    - The "bb" is a short "b" sound.
    - The "age" sounds like "ij" or the "idge" in "ridge."
The pronunciation of **cabbage** generally remains consistent in both strong and weak forms, as it's a content word. There isn't a significant reduction in the vowel sounds in connected speech.
## Synonyms
### For "a type of vegetable"
- **Cole:** An older or more botanical term for cabbage or similar leafy vegetables (e.g., in "coleslaw").
    - _Example:_ Traditional **cole** crops include cabbage and kale.
- **Head cabbage:** (Specifically for the common headed variety)
    - _Example:_ She bought a large **head cabbage** for the soup.
### For "severely brain-damaged person" (Offensive)
- **Vegetable:** (Highly offensive) Used to describe a person who has lost most brain function.
    - _Example:_ It's a derogatory term to call a person a **vegetable**.
### For "money" (Slang)
- **Cash:** Actual currency in the form of notes or coins.
    - _Example:_ Do you have any **cash** on you?
- **Dough:** (Informal) Money.
    - _Example:_ He's got plenty of **dough** saved up.
- **Greens:** (Slang) Money, especially paper money (less common than "cabbage" for this meaning).
    - _Example:_ He's always chasing the **greens**.
### For "scraps of cloth" (Obsolete/Archaic)
- **Remnants:** Remaining small pieces or quantities.
    - _Example:_ She used the fabric **remnants** to make a patchwork quilt.
- **Scraps:** Small pieces or amounts of something.
    - _Example:_ The tailor collected the **scraps** of fabric.
### For "to steal or pilfer" (Verb)
- **Pilfer:** To steal (typically things of relatively little value).
    - _Example:_ He would **pilfer** small items from the shop.
- **Filch:** To steal (something, especially something of small value) in a casual way.
    - _Example:_ She managed to **filch** a few cookies from the jar.
- **Swipe:** (Informal) To steal.
    - _Example:_ Someone tried to **swipe** my phone.
## Lexical equivalent
The word **cabbage** has lexical equivalents in many languages, though the informal/offensive meanings might not have direct, single-word translations.
### Spanish
The word **cabbage** has lexical equivalents like "col" or "repollo" (for the vegetable). For the slang meanings, there aren't direct equivalents; "dinero" (money) or "persona en estado vegetativo" (person in a vegetative state) would be used.
### German
The word **cabbage** has lexical equivalents like "Kohl" (for the vegetable). For the offensive meaning, "Vegetarier" (a term for someone in a vegetative state, but still offensive) might be used. For money, "Geld" is used.
### French
The word **cabbage** has lexical equivalents like "chou" (for the vegetable). For the offensive meaning, "légume" (vegetable, used similarly offensively) or "personne dans le coma" (person in a coma) could be used. For money, "argent" is used.
### Italian
The word **cabbage** has lexical equivalents like "cavolo" (for the vegetable). For the offensive meaning, "vegetale" (vegetable, used offensively) or "persona in coma" (person in a coma) could be used. For money, "denaro" or "soldi" is used.
