# Chunky
The word **chunky** generally describes something that is thick, solid, and heavy, or that contains large, thick pieces. It can also describe a person who is somewhat fat or stocky.
## Meaning
### Thick, solid, and heavy
This meaning of **chunky** refers to objects or materials that are noticeably thick, robust, or substantial in form.
#### Examples
> "She wore a **chunky** knit sweater." This means the sweater was made of thick, heavy material. "The furniture had **chunky** legs." This means the furniture legs were thick and solid.
### Containing large pieces
When describing food or mixtures, **chunky** indicates that there are distinct, sizable pieces within a softer or smoother base.
#### Examples
> "I prefer **chunky** peanut butter over smooth." This means the peanut butter contains noticeable bits of peanuts. "The soup was **chunky** with vegetables." This means the soup had large pieces of vegetables in it.
### Stocky or somewhat fat (of a person)
When applied to a person, **chunky** describes someone who is broad, well-built, or a bit on the heavier side. It can be used as a polite way to say someone is chubby or stocky.
#### Examples
> "He was a **chunky** football player with a strong build." This means he was stocky and muscular. "The baby had **chunky** arms and legs." This means the baby had plump, thick arms and legs.
## Phonetic and pronunciation
The pronunciation of **chunky** is /ˈtʃʌŋki/ (American English) or /ˈtʃʌŋki/ (British English).
- **Sounds:**
	- The "ch" sound is like in "chair."
	- The "u" sound is like in "fun" or "cup."
	- The "nk" sound is a hard "n" followed by a hard "k."
	- The "y" at the end makes an "ee" sound like in "happy."
The word **chunky** generally maintains a consistent pronunciation, whether in isolation or connected speech, as its syllables are stressed relatively equally, and the sounds are clear. There isn't a distinct "weak form" as seen in some function words.
