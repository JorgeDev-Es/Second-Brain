# Further

The word **further** primarily functions as an adverb meaning "to a greater distance or degree," and also as an adjective meaning "more; additional" or "more distant." It can also be used as a verb meaning "to help the progress of (something)."

## Meaning

### To a greater distance, extent, or degree

This usage of **further** indicates moving to a more advanced point in space, time, or an abstract measure. It can refer to physical distance, but also to progress in an argument, a task, or a concept. It's used as an adverb.

#### Examples

> "Let's discuss this **further** at our next meeting." This means "Let's discuss this to a greater extent at our next meeting." "She moved **further** into the shadows." This means "She moved to a greater distance into the shadows."

### More; additional

When used this way, **further** means "more" or "additional." It refers to something extra or supplementary. It's used as an adjective.

#### Examples

> "Do you have any **further** questions?" This means "Do you have any additional questions?" "For **further** information, please contact us." This means "For more information, please contact us."

### More distant or advanced

This usage of **further** refers to something that is more remote in space or time, or more advanced in a sequence. It's used as an adjective.

#### Examples

> "The **further** side of the mountain was unexplored." This means "The more distant side of the mountain was unexplored." "They discussed the **further** implications of the decision." This means "They discussed the more advanced implications of the decision."

### To help the progress of (something)

When used as a verb, **further** means to promote or advance something, to help it develop or succeed. It's used as a verb.

#### Examples

> "The new policy aims to **further** economic growth." This means "The new policy aims to help the progress of economic growth." "He dedicated his life to **furthering** scientific research." This means "He dedicated his life to helping the progress of scientific research."

## Phonetic and pronunciation

The pronunciation of **further** is generally consistent without distinct strong or weak forms, but the vowel sound can vary slightly between American and British English.

- **Pronunciation:**
    - American English: /ˈfɜrðər/
    - British English: /ˈfɜːðə/
- **Sounds:**
    - The "f" sound is as in "fan."
    - The "ur" sound is typically a rhotic 'er' sound in American English and a non-rhotic 'er' sound in British English.
    - The "th" sound is a voiced "th" like in "this" or "them."
    - The final "er" sound is a schwa /ər/ in American English and a non-rhotic schwa /ə/ in British English.

## Synonyms

### Synonyms of "Further" (as an Adverb: to a greater degree/distance)

- **More:** This is a very common and often interchangeable synonym, especially when referring to quantity or degree.
    - _Example:_ "Can you explain this **more** clearly?"
- **Additionally:** Indicates something extra or in addition to what has already been mentioned.
    - _Example:_ "She provided the main points, and then spoke **additionally** about the details."
- **Beyond:** Implies going past a certain point or limit.
    - _Example:_ "He pushed himself **beyond** his limits."
- **Deeper:** Refers to exploring something in more detail or with more complexity.
    - _Example:_ "Let's delve **deeper** into the topic."
- **Onward:** Implies moving forward in space or time.
    - _Example:_ "From this point **onward**, we will proceed cautiously."

### Synonyms of "Further" (as an Adjective: additional/more distant)

- **Additional:** The most direct synonym when referring to something extra or added.
    - _Example:_ "We need **additional** resources."
- **More:** Very common and generally interchangeable, especially when referring to quantity.
    - _Example:_ "Do you need **more** time?"
- **Extra:** Implies something beyond what is usual, expected, or necessary.
    - _Example:_ "She packed some **extra** clothes."
- **Supplementary:** Refers to something that completes or enhances something else.
    - _Example:_ "The book comes with **supplementary** materials."
- **Other:** Can sometimes be used to mean "another" or "different," implying something additional.
    - _Example:_ "Are there any **other** options?"
- **Distant:** When referring to physical distance.
    - _Example:_ "The **distant** mountains were covered in snow."
- **Remote:** Similar to distant, often implying isolated or far away.
    - _Example:_ "They lived in a **remote** village."

### Synonyms of "Further" (as a Verb: to help the progress of)

- **Advance:** To make progress or cause something to progress.
    - _Example:_ "His research helped to **advance** our understanding of the disease."
- **Promote:** To support or encourage the progress or development of something.
    - _Example:_ "The organization works to **promote** peace."
- **Develop:** To grow or cause to grow and become more mature, advanced, or elaborate.
    - _Example:_ "Education helps to **develop** critical thinking skills."
- **Aid:** To help or assist.
    - _Example:_ "The grant will **aid** scientific discovery."
- **Foster:** To encourage the development of something good.
    - _Example:_ "We aim to **foster** a sense of community."
- **Strengthen:** To make or become stronger.
    - _Example:_ "The new policies are designed to **strengthen** the economy."
